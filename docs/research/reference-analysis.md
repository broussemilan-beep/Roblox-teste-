# Reference study v2: an 11 s Roblox TikTok animation (user-supplied screen recording)

Source: a phone screen recording of a TikTok post ("Short animation bc i was bored", account `dayume`). Not redistributed:
only observations and measurements. v1 of this file was written from sparse contact sheets and was wrong on several points
(scale of the effects, the avatar's design, how the juggle is built, what the storm looks like). v2 comes from a frame-by-frame
re-read of all 336 picture frames at 30 fps (the last ~10 frames of the recording are the iPhone control centre, not the clip).

Tags: `observed` = seen on the frames, `measured` = counted/estimated from pixels, `inferred` = my reading.

## 1. The avatar is not "basic blocks": it is basic *rigging* with a rich silhouette

`observed`. Both characters are plain Roblox R6 rigs, but the hero wears accessories that give it a large, readable
silhouette from any angle: a wide round-brim black hat with a tall crown and a band, a big shock of **white hair** (several
long locks, covers one eye), a **long black coat with flared tails**, a belt with white stripes, white cuffs, round
glasses, and a white **speech-bubble sticker ("...") on the hat**. Limbs are plain black blocks. The dummy is the default
white R6 with a smiley (a frown when hurt).

Consequence: "the level" is *silhouette design + staging*, not the rig. A thin R6 with a tiny hat (my v2) loses on screen.

## 2. Staging (camera) - `observed`

* Combat wide shots are **pure profile** (camera perpendicular to the fight axis), slightly elevated at the start (horizon in the
  top fifth), later **ground level** (horizon in the bottom third, bright sky gradient above). The characters are
  **35-50 % of the frame height** in wide shots and **60-75 %** in the ground-level juggle.
* The camera **pans to keep the action**: the dummy is flung to the right edge, the hero stays left; in the lull the hero slides to
  the right edge while the dummy leaves through the top. Fighters never sit small in the middle of an empty frame.
* Hard cuts between wide, macro, ground-level and sky shots; there is no continuous dolly through the fight.
* Macros are real 3D cameras 0.5-1 stud from a part, with Roblox lighting (soft highlights on box edges).

## 4. What matters for animation (`inferred` from the above)

1. **Pose design for the camera.** Contact poses are drawn as flat silhouettes in profile (long diagonals, a tucked ball for spins).
2. **Hold the hero, animate the victim.** The 33-frame juggle is a held hero plus a 3-4 pose dummy cycle plus persistent ink.
3. **Graphic fx are *big*** (the black crescent is as long as the hero, the juggle swirls are half his size, storm strokes are 15-25 % of the
   frame) and **opaque black or white**, not thin or translucent. They are part of the composition.
4. **The victim is a straight board** and gets re-placed between hits (it stands again at the right edge).
5. **Dislocation exists** (frame 141: the arm block is clearly detached from the body for a couple of frames).
6. **Punctuation**: 2f white spikes, 2f black, 1f 4-point glint, 3-4f manga impact frames (twice, with cyan/red accents only inside them).
7. **The dummy and the hero are separated by contrast** (white vs black); the middle grey floor and sky keep both readable.
8. Pace as contrast: runs of 1-2 frame shots (storm, impacts) against 10-48 frame holds.

## 5. What my film (v2) still gets wrong against this (honest list)

* The hero is a thin R6 with a small hat and a few hair strands: no volume, so he reads as a skinny figure and the face is a generic smile.
* Wide shots are 3/4 with a long lens look; the reference is profile, ground-level when the action is a juggle, and tight.
* The juggle re-poses the hero every 4 frames and the ink there is faint; the reference holds the hero and cycles the dummy.
* My ink trails are 1.5 studs wide and grey-translucent; the reference's are black, opaque, tapered and body-length.
* My storm is 3D strokes a couple of studs wide that I thinned to keep poses readable; the reference deliberately buries the bodies.
* The final shot is a small standing hero; the reference is a 48-frame macro on a hunched hero, hat dome, cuff block and the dummy's arm.
